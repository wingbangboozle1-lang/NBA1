Executive summary read first

Five things worth knowing before any decision.

  1. The line model is not distinguishable from a naive pick. On the margin there is something: mean absolute error 12.913 points across 60 graded finals against 14.886 for predicting one constant, a gain of 1.973 points. On the total there is nothing: 14.636 against 14.658 for a constant, i.e. the pace-based total is 0.022 points WORSE than doing no work. And every component — home court, back-to-back, shrinkage — moves the margin error by less than half a point, inside a ±2.5 interval. This is reported as the finding, not smoothed over.
  2. The binding constraint on picks is the injury report, not the ratings. Where one side had filed and the other had not, an adjustment built from one team's absences and the other team's silence is a bias — those games are priced and then refused (5 today). Dates where nobody has filed yet are published as provisional lines (8), and 9 more sit inside the model's own noise.
  3. Rotation impact cannot be sized yet, and that is now a measured gap. 1 of the 26 listed absences in the newest edition (Injury Report: 11/14/26 05:00 AM) have verified season minutes anywhere in this repository. Vacated minutes are deducted from the named player and deliberately not re-spread to teammates, because the data to do that fairly (page 2 onward of the player tables) was never captured.
  4. Dispersion stopped being an assumption. Player-prop σ used to be a constant in this repo, which is how ±30-point spreads appeared in an earlier draft. 12 official game-log rows now give a measured spread for 3 players, published with its interval and flagged as far too small to stake.
  5. Nothing here is a bet. No sportsbook price is reachable from the sandbox that produced these files, so break-even, hold, ROI and closing-line value are blank on every row and will stay blank until a price file exists. A paper forecast graded against its own number is not an edge.
