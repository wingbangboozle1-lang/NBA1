The one number that decides everything else

The published picks, graded against the official finals in data/settlement/**

The model is confident where it is wrong

('

Over 37 decided paper singles the record is 14-23-1 — a hit rate of 37.8% against a 52.38% break-even at the disclosed -110 paper price, for -11.30 units. On those same tickets the model published a mean win probability of 0.720: it was 34.1 points overconfident.

The most confident bucket is the worst bucket. At a published probability of 0.70 or higher the model went 7-11 while claiming 79.5%.

Why — measured, not guessed
The probability divides the model\'s spread by the standard deviation of game margins (16.71 points). The quantity that belongs there is the standard deviation of the error in the model\'s own spread, which on these settled games is 16.09, with a mean bias of +8.72 points — the model over-predicts the picked team\'s winning margin. The pick gate keeps only |spread| ≥ 3.0, so it keeps exactly the games where that bias is largest.
What this does not prove: that the model has no information. The smallest bucket (3.0–5.0) went 4-3 at the model\'s own number. What it proves is that the confidence is miscalibrated and the selection rule amplifies the error. Both are fixable and the fix is measurable.

Sigma used by the model: 16.714. Sigma implied by its own published probabilities: 16.595. Sigma needed to cover its measured error: 18.303 (9.5% more). Sources: signal diagnostics, settlement 11/18.

The first two re-read the data files in this repository and print the tables above. The official values they compare against are typed into the verifier, one per page, each with the URL and the UTC read time, so a reader can check a value against the live page by eye. A value that was not printed on the page is left out of the verifier rather than guessed, and the comparator reports it as unverified.
