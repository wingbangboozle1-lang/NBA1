Why the error column can look large. A single NBA game's margin is a high-variance quantity: the residual standard deviation here is about 17.4 points. An average absolute error near 13 points is normal and is not a defect — which is exactly why one game proves nothing and 60 games barely prove anything.

Sign convention. A published margin is from the home team's point of view, so a positive number means the home side is favoured. Every error column is signed: actual − predicted. A negative error therefore means the home team did worse than predicted, not that the prediction was better — a −40 is a 40-point miss. The Show filter compares absolute errors, and the averages on the Validation page are means of absolute values. Each row links to the official game page; a large total error on both sides of the same game is typical of an overtime or a blowout.
