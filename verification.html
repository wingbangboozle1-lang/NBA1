It proves that these URLs answered (or refused) at 2026-11-14T01:10:03Z from a GitHub-hosted runner, and that the three injury-report PDFs cited by this site were retrievable and parseable at that time. It does not prove that any figure in this repository is current, correctly parsed, or still valid tomorrow. Team efficiency ratings, player statistics, the schedule and the injury-report PDFs are all retrievable without a browser. Before this probe, the repository could only say this path was untested.

2. Two sources stay closed, and one of them matters

ak-static.cms.nba.com served the 11/13 injury PDFs but returned 403 for the 11/14 edition; separate evidence says that file does not exist yet, so this is the source being absent rather than the source being closed. cdn.nba.com/static/json/... returned 403 both ways — that endpoint is not usable from a script at all, and any flag or note that leans on it should be treated as unconfirmed.

3. Technical reachability is not permission

This is the most important caveat on the page. Reaching a page by imitating its own client is a fact about HTTP, not a right to use. Nothing in this probe establishes a licence, terms of use, rate limit, or permission to store or republish NBA data, and this page does not claim otherwise. It is recorded so that the feasibility question can be answered with evidence; the permission question is still open and belongs to a human, on Questions & suggestions (Q-02, Q-08, and the legal review Q-04).

4. No cited source was dead

0 cited URLs returned 404/410 and 0 were unreachable. Every source link on this site pointed at a URL that still existed at probe time, so no published table row rests on a citation that has since vanished.
