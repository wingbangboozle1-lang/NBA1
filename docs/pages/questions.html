Three questions block everything. What price can the bets be placed at (Q01), how large a graded sample exists (Q02), and whether the ratings were captured before the games they predict (Q03). The first has no answer inside this environment; the second and third are answered by time and by a daily snapshot, not by more analysis.

Q01BLOCKINGWhat price can the bets actually be placed at?No sportsbook or exchange price was observable anywhere in this environment. Every ROI figure in this project assumes a standard -110 price.A licensed two-sided price feed, or the account's actual available odds.An edge measured against an assumed price is not an edge. A strategy that returns +4% at -110 can be -3% at -105. Until real prices exist, no strategy can be validated, sized, or rewarded.Licensed odds API (e.g. a paid sportsbook feed) or a manual export of account odds. This is the single largest blocker in the project.source
Q02BLOCKINGHow many graded bets are needed, and how long will it take?The gate floor is 100 graded bets. The largest sample available here is 14 games.A forward-tested sample. No amount of analysis on 14 games substitutes for it.Statistical gates cannot be met by re-analysis. The only route is passing time with logged pre-game predictions.Run the pipeline daily before tip-off, log every originated line and every pick, and grade them after the finals. At roughly 8 games a night and one pick per game, 100 graded bets is about two weeks.source
Q03BLOCKINGWere the team ratings captured before or after the games being predicted?They were captured on 11/13 and therefore include the 11/11 and 11/12 games used in the backtest.Point-in-time snapshots of every team's rating as of the morning of each game.The current backtest is look-ahead contaminated. Its 78.6% straight-up rate is inflated and cannot be presented as skill.Snapshot the team stats tables each morning into a dated file. This project already writes dated data files, so the change is to keep one per day.source
Q04IMPORTANTWhat is the real game-to-game dispersion of totals and margins?Measured from 14 games: total sd 17.13, margin sd 16.13, mean total 226.31.A few hundred games, and ideally a per-team split, since fast teams have wider totals than slow ones.A 14-game standard deviation has enormous sampling error. Q05IMPORTANTHow much of a missing player's production actually leaves the team?The model assumes 55% transfers away, with a sensitivity band from 35% to 75%.A measured value, ideally split by player archetype (creator, rim protector, shooter).On 11/13 the assumption alone moves GSW@SAS from 218.5 to 207.3 in total, and the spread from 13.3 to 24.5. That range is wider than any plausible edge.For players who missed games, compare team OffRtg in games with and without them, using official team game logs. Q06IMPORTANTWhat is the home-court advantage for this season?Net zero in the 14-game sample (mean home margin -0.14), which is pure noise at that size.A season-level HCA estimate, and ideally a per-team one.The model applies no HCA at all. That systematically underprices every home team, which is a bias, not a neutral choice.Regress margin on a home indicator over several hundred games, using the same finals corpus.source
Q07IMPORTANTDo same-game parlay legs actually co-move, and by how much?Six candidate correlations were identified and left explicitly null (correlations.json tier C). Publishing one would be fabrication.Pull per-game player box scores for the season (NBA.com publishes them per player), join to finals, and compute the pairwise correlations directly.source
