Why there are two registers. Two independently produced question sets exist on this repository and they overlap only partly, so both are published. Register B — quantitative modelling

Questions whose answers change a number, a gate or a conclusion in the model.

Three questions block everything. What price can the bets be placed at (Q01), how large a graded sample exists (Q02), and whether the ratings were captured before the games they predict (Q03). The first has no answer inside this environment; the second and third are answered by time and by a daily snapshot, not by more analysis.

Q01BLOCKINGWhat price can the bets actually be placed at?No sportsbook or exchange price was observable anywhere in this environment. Every ROI figure in this project assumes a standard -110 price.A licensed two-sided price feed, or the account's actual available odds.An edge measured against an assumed price is not an edge. A strategy that returns +4% at -110 can be -3% at -105. Until real prices exist, no strategy can be validated, sized, or rewarded.Licensed odds API (e.g. a paid sportsbook feed) or a manual export of account odds. This is the single largest blocker in the project.source
Q02BLOCKINGHow many graded bets are needed, and how long will it take?The gate floor is 100 graded bets. The largest sample available here is 14 games.A forward-tested sample. No amount of analysis on 14 games substitutes for it.Statistical gates cannot be met by re-analysis. The only route is passing time with logged pre-game predictions.Run the pipeline daily before tip-off, log every originated line and every pick, and grade them after the finals. At roughly 8 games a night and one pick per game, 100 graded bets is about two weeks.source
Q03BLOCKINGWere the team ratings captured before or after the games being predicted?They were captured on 11/13 and therefore include the 11/11 and 11/12 games used in the backtest.Point-in-time snapshots of every team's rating as of the morning of each game.The current backtest is look-ahead contaminated. Its 78.6% straight-up rate is inflated and cannot be presented as skill.Snapshot the team stats tables each morning into a dated file. This project already writes dated data files, so the change is to keep one per day.source
Q04IMPORTANTWhat is the real game-to-game dispersion of totals and margins?Measured from 14 games: total sd 17.13, margin sd 16.13, mean total 226.31.A few hundred games, and ideally a per-team split, since fast teams have wider totals than slow ones.A 14-game standard deviation has enormous sampling error. Every probability in the model scales off it.Accumulate finals from official box scores. 200 games would give a usable estimate; the same logger that solves Q02 solves this.source
Q05IMPORTANTHow much of a missing player's production actually leaves the team?The model assumes 55% transfers away, with a sensitivity band from 35% to 75%.A measured value, ideally split by player archetype (creator, rim protector, shooter).On 11/13 the assumption alone moves GSW@SAS from 218.5 to 207.3 in total, and the spread from 13.3 to 24.5. That range is wider than any plausible edge.For players who missed games, compare team OffRtg in games with and without them, using official team game logs. Requires the per-game data the project cannot yet pull.source
Q06IMPORTANTWhat is the home-court advantage for this season?Net zero in the 14-game sample (mean home margin -0.14), which is pure noise at that size.A season-level HCA estimate, and ideally a per-team one.The model applies no HCA at all. That systematically underprices every home team, which is a bias, not a neutral choice.Regress margin on a home indicator over several hundred games, using the same finals corpus.source
Q07IMPORTANTDo same-game parlay legs actually co-move, and by how much?Six candidate correlations were identified and left explicitly null (correlations.json tier C). Only cross-team season aggregates could be measured here.A player game-log corpus joined to final scores.Without the joint distribution, any same-game parlay price is invented. Publishing one would be fabrication.Pull per-game player box scores for the season (NBA.com publishes them per player), join to finals, and compute the pairwise correlations directly.source
Q08IMPORTANTWhich players on the injury report have already been out for a while?Ten players were verified individually. Getting this wrong in the other direction ignores genuine news.Systematically read the 'Last 5 Games' table on each player page, or use the official per-game logs.source

Q11INFORMATIONALShould the NBA Cup games be treated differently?All nine games on 11/13 are Emirates NBA Cup group play.Any evidence that Cup games differ in pace, effort, or rotation.It does not block, but if Cup games differ, pooling them with regular games biases the dispersion estimate.Compare scoring and pace in Cup vs non-Cup games once both samples exist.source
Q12INFORMATIONALAre the rotation minutes for teams correct at 240 per game?240 is 5 players x 48 minutes, fixed by Rules 3 and 5.Nothing on the rule side. What is missing is the actual distribution of those minutes across each roster.It does not block the current model, which redistributes proportionally. It would block any attempt to forecast individual player minutes.Official per-game box scores give every player's minutes directly.source
