Game flow

What a 48-minute NBA game can physically look like, and which of those constraints this model actually uses. The rules below are quoted from the official rulebook, and each is tied to the number it fixes in the model.

Why this page exists. A projected total is a claim about how many possessions two teams will get and how efficiently they convert them. Possessions are bounded by the shot clock, the period length and the substitution rules. If a projection implies more possessions than the rules allow, the projection is wrong regardless of the statistics behind it.

Possession arithmetic

League pace this season is 99.853 possessions per 48 minutes. That is consistent with a 24-second clock: 48 minutes is 2,880 seconds, and at roughly 14–16 seconds of actual possession per trip a team lands near 100 possessions. The 14-second reset after an offensive rebound is the reason pace sits above 96 rather than below it.

Regulation seconds
48 × 60 = 2,880
Shot clock
24 seconds, with 14 seconds after an offensive rebound (Rule 7 IV)
Overtime
5 minutes per extra period (Rule 5 III) — excluded from the total dispersion estimate because an OT game is not the same event
Halftime
15 minutes (Rule 5 IV)
Timeouts
7 per regulation game, at most 4 in the fourth quarter, 2 in overtime (Rule 5 VI)

Flow effects that are not modelled

  • Garbage time. A blowout shortens the effective game and drags totals under. No official source publishes a garbage-time adjustment, so none is applied.
  • Foul-game endgames. Deliberate fouling adds possessions in the last two minutes and inflates totals. Not modelled.
  • Timeout usage. The allowance is known; its effect on pace is not, and no official data ties the two.
  • Back-to-backs and travel. The schedule shows the dates but no official rest-effect estimate exists, so rest is not in the model.
  • Overtime probability. Roughly one game in fifteen reaches overtime in this sample (1 of 14), but that is far too small a sample to price, and OT games are excluded from the dispersion estimate rather than predicted.
