Backtest — and why it cannot be trusted

The model was run against the 14 games that finished on 11/11 and 11/12. The pipeline works. The result is not evidence of skill, and the label below is the most important thing on this page.

CONTAMINATED — inputs are season-to-date and include the tested games. The team ratings used as inputs were captured on 11/13, which means they include the very games being predicted. That is look-ahead bias. A clean walk-forward test needs each team's rating as of the morning of the game, and those snapshots were never taken.

What the numbers do and do not say. The totals model beat a league-average baseline by 2.04 points of mean absolute error, which is a real improvement in fit. But the 78.6% straight-up rate is inflated by the contamination, and 14 games is far too few to distinguish 79% from a coin flip that ran hot. The honest reading is: the mechanics are sound, the validation is pending.

Lessons the errors actually teach

  • The single biggest miss is an overtime game. DET@WAS finished 284 points after two overtimes against a 216.1 projection, a 67.9-point error. This is why overtime games are excluded from the dispersion estimate rather than predicted.
  • Two large misses went the same direction. BKN@NOP (+23.3) and ORL@UTA (+15.4) both beat their projections badly, suggesting the model under-reacts to fast, high-variance teams.
  • Close calls are frequent. 5 of 14 games finished within 2 points of the projected margin, which is roughly what noise would produce.
  • ATS accuracy against our own line is 57.1% — below break-even. Against a line we generated ourselves, that is a warning, not a result.

Verdict

NOT VALIDATION. Look-ahead bias is present because the team stats cover the same games being predicted. Use this to confirm the pipeline runs, not to claim skill.
