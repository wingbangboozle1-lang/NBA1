What the data says

Ten strategies were written, seven validation gates were defined, and all ten were run against a strictly out-of-sample window: all 51 completed games from 2026-11-06 to 2026-11-12, every one predicted using team ratings computed only from 2026-10-20 to 2026-11-05. No game in the test window contributes to the ratings that predict it. On top of that the system now originates a line for every game in the next three days - 14, 15, 16 and 17 November - from official ratings and the newest official injury report.

1. The model wins on the margin and loses on totals and winners

Margin: MAE 12.89 against 13.19 for the naive reference, RMSE 17.10 against 17.70 - the model is better on both. Totals: MAE 14.86 against 14.75, RMSE 19.88 against 19.59 - the reference is better on both. Straight up both went 37-14, but the reference has the better Brier score (0.1889 against 0.1970) and the better log loss (0.5567 against 0.5768).

Reading: The refinements buy a real but tiny margin improvement and nothing else. The probability calibration is the weakest part of the model, and it is where the remaining work should go.

2. Growing the window from 28 games to 51 changed a conclusion - which is the most useful thing that happened

On the 28-game window the model beat the reference on totals (MAE 14.50 against 14.75). On 51 games it does not (14.86 against 14.75). The sign flipped. That is exactly the failure mode the sample gate exists to catch, and it is why nothing here is being promoted on the earlier reading.

Reading: Any single-window result at this sample size should be treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.

3. The best strategy is 16-7, and that is not enough

S1 (side edge vs the reference spread) produced 23 graded plays at 16-7, worth +7.55 model units and a 32.8% return at -110. Its exact binomial p-value against a coin flip is 0.093. The Wilson 95% interval on its hit rate is 0.491 to 0.844, and the lower bound is below the 0.5238 break-even, so the skill gate fails. S5 (back-to-back fade) is 8-4 on 12 plays, also short.

Reading: A 69.6% hit rate on 23 plays is a promising signal and nothing more. The leaderboard caps it at quarter-unit regardless of its score, which is the designed behaviour.

4. Home court looks normal; the back-to-back penalty is large but uncertain

Home-court advantage estimates at +2.79 points with a standard error of 2.48 (t = 1.12), squarely in the historical NBA range. The back-to-back penalty estimates at -6.10 points with a standard error of 4.87 (t = -1.25); because |t| >= 1 the model applies the full 6.10, but the standard error is most of the estimate and detection is still incomplete for 2026-11-06, which biases it toward zero.

Reading: Both are published with their standard errors and both are fitted on the test window, which is disclosed on every row they touch.

5. Shrinkage was derived rather than guessed, and the curve is flat where it matters

The empirical-Bayes calculation gives k = 2.9, weighting each team's observed net rating at 0.739. The fixed-k sweep shows margin MAE moving only from 13.28 at k = 0 to 12.91 at k = 4, then degrading to 13.65 by k = 20. Heavy shrinkage is clearly wrong; the choice between light and none barely matters.

Reading: The constant is defensible, its sensitivity is published, and it is not a source of hidden risk.

6. Availability impact is a measured floor, not an estimate

Minutes are only known for the 50 players in the captured official table. Players outside it contribute 0.00 to the impact, so teams missing rotation players who are not top-50 scorers show a smaller number than the truth - sometimes zero. The minutes-coverage column sits next to every figure so a 0.00 is never read as 'healthy'.

Reading: Correct but incomplete - the Injuries page says so on every row rather than hiding it.

7. Correlation is measured where it can be and flagged where it cannot

Thirteen team-statistic pairs were measured directly on all 30 teams from the official advanced table. Net rating explains 87% of the variation in win percentage; pace is nearly unrelated to net rating (r = -0.34, p = 0.063) and assist percentage is unrelated to offensive efficiency (r = 0.02, p = 0.92). The parlay and same-game-parlay correlations that drive pricing are model-derived and labelled as such.

Reading: The distinction matters most for parlays, because pricing correlated legs as independent is the classic way to overpay.

8. The word 'edge' on this site does not mean edge against a sportsbook

No sportsbook price is published by an NBA-operated source, so no official link can be attached to one. The site bets the originated line against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. Until real prices are logged, 'units won' means model-vs-naive-model units.

Reading: This is the single most important caveat for anyone reading the leaderboard.

Reading: Two things are not yet knowable and are published as such rather than filled in. The 11/13 finals were not published when the capture was made, so the ratings window still ends at 11/12 and 18 team records are one night out of date. And at the 07:30 PM edition five of the ten teams playing on 11/14 had not filed an injury report, while no edition yet covers the 11/15 to 11/17 games. Those lines carry no injury adjustment and say so.

How to read this site

Every row has a source

Each table row carries a source id that links to the official page or PDF it came from. Click any S1-style link to check it yourself.

Three kinds of number

official published by the NBA · derived arithmetic or model on official inputs · assumption a modelling choice, listed in full.

Blanks are deliberate

n/a means no official source value existed. Nothing was filled in. Those gaps are catalogued on the Flagged page.

Bottom line

The machinery works end to end: it originates lines for every game three days out, it tests ten strategies against a real out-of-sample window, it refuses to promote anything that has not cleared the skill gate, and it publishes every gap it cannot close. What it has not yet done is prove that any of it is skill. That is the next piece of work, and it needs one thing this repository cannot reach from an official source: a price.
