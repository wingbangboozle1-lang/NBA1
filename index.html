What this site is, and what it is not

This is an origination and research workspace. It produces its own lines from official NBA data and then tries hard to prove that those lines carry skill. As of this build no strategy has cleared the skill gate: the best one is 23-9 at Tier 3 - Quarter unit, which is inside what luck alone produces. Every strategy is therefore capped at a quarter unit or held back as research-only, and that is the result, not an obstacle to be worked around.

No sportsbook price is used anywhere, because no NBA-operated source publishes one. “Edge” on this site means the model against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. It does not mean an edge against a book.

Executive summary

The horizon runs the full three days: 27 games from 14 to 17 November 2026, with an originated line, a paper pick and an injury read-out for every one, and a source link on every row. The 2026-11-13 finals are now captured, settled and folded into a 60-game out-of-sample window, and the newest official injury-report edition (11/14/26 04:15 AM ET) has been transcribed row by row. The honest read: a small edge on the margin, none on totals or winners, and one strategy whose record has cleared the luck gate at last (S1, 23-9, p = 0.020) - but not the calibration and stability gates, so it stays capped at a quarter unit.

Ten strategies were written, seven validation gates were defined, and all ten were run against a strictly out-of-sample window: all 60 completed games from 2026-11-06 to 2026-11-13, every one predicted using team ratings computed only from 2026-10-20 to 2026-11-05. No game in the test window contributes to the ratings that predict it. On top of that the system originates a line for every game in the next three days - 14, 15, 16 and 17 November - from official ratings and the newest official injury report.

Verdict — PROVISIONAL - not funded
The model beats the naive reference on margin accuracy (MAE 12.98 against 13.62) and loses to it on totals (14.83 against 14.72) and on picking winners (42-18 against 40-20). S1 (side edge) is now 23-9, worth +11.91 model units, and for the first time its record is outside what luck alone produces (exact binomial p = 0.020, Wilson lower bound 0.546 against the 0.524 break-even) - but it fails the calibration gate (hit rate 0.719 against a mean predicted 0.627) and the stability gate (the bootstrap interval on the model's margin MAE still overlaps the reference), and the availability gate cannot run at all. Under the tier ceilings it stays a quarter-unit strategy. The 27 new lines are published as lines and paper picks, not as recommendations.
Why: Sixty games with a 17.4-point residual standard deviation have finally made one record statistically distinguishable from a coin flip - and that is exactly when the other gates matter most. A hit rate the model itself did not predict is a calibration failure, not a windfall; an improvement whose interval overlaps the reference is not stable; and a gate that cannot run is not passed. Selective skill means paying for the part that survives every gate and nothing else.
  1. The model wins on the margin and loses on totals and winners — Margin: MAE 12.98 against 13.62 for the naive reference, RMSE 17.12 against 17.88 - the model is better on both. Totals: MAE 14.83 against 14.72, RMSE 19.35 against 19.10 - the reference is better on both. Straight up the reference went 42-18 and the model 40-20, and the reference has the better Brier score (0.2001 against 0.2020) and the better log loss (0.5827 against 0.5873).
    The refinements buy a real but tiny margin improvement and nothing else. The probability calibration is the weakest part of the model, and it is where the remaining work should go.
  2. Growing the window keeps changing what can be claimed - which is the point of the sample gate — On the 28-game window the model beat the reference on totals (MAE 14.50 against 14.75). On 51 games it did not (14.86 against 14.75) - the sign flipped. On the 60-game window the sign holds (14.83 against 14.72) and the gap narrowed, but the conclusion stands. Each addition of data has moved at least one claim.
    Any single-window result at this sample size should be treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.
  3. S1 has cleared the luck gate at 23-9 - and failed two others, which is the honest headline — S1 (side edge vs the reference spread) produced 32 graded plays at 23-9, worth +11.91 model units and a 37.2% return at -110. Its exact binomial p-value against a coin flip is 0.020 and the Wilson 95% interval on its hit rate is 0.546 to 0.844 - the lower bound is now above the 0.5238 break-even, so G4 (skill, not luck) passes for the first time. But its hit rate of 0.719 against a mean predicted probability of 0.627 fails the calibration gate (gap 0.091 against a 0.08 tolerance), and the stability gate fails because the bootstrap interval on the model's margin MAE (10.2 to 15.94) still contains the reference MAE (13.62). The availability gate cannot run at all for the graded window.
    This is what selectively skill-based looks like: one gate says this is probably not luck, two gates say the model does not understand why, and the funding tier stays at quarter unit until every gate that can be run has been run and passed.
  4. Home court looks normal; the back-to-back penalty is large but uncertain — Home-court advantage estimates at +3.51 points with a standard error of 2.28 (t = 1.54) across 60 games, squarely in the historical NBA range. The back-to-back penalty estimates at -6.26 points with a standard error of 4.86 (t = -1.29); because |t| >= 1 the model applies the full 6.26, but the standard error is most of the estimate.
    Both are published with their standard errors and both are fitted on the test window, which is disclosed on every row they touch.
  5. Shrinkage was derived rather than guessed, and the curve is flat where it matters — The empirical-Bayes calculation gives k = 2.9, weighting each team's observed net rating at 0.739. The fixed-k sweep shows margin MAE moving from 13.62 at k = 0 to 12.91 at k = 4, then degrading to 13.36 by k = 20. Heavy shrinkage is clearly wrong; the choice between light and none barely matters.
    The constant is defensible, its sensitivity is published, and it is not a source of hidden risk.
  6. Availability impact is a measured floor, not an estimate — Minutes are only known for the 50 players in the captured official table. Players outside it contribute 0.00 to the impact, so teams missing rotation players who are not top-50 scorers show a smaller number than the truth - sometimes zero. The minutes-coverage column sits next to every figure so a 0.00 is never read as 'healthy'.
    Correct but incomplete - the Injuries page says so on every row rather than hiding it.
  7. Correlation is measured where it can be and flagged where it cannot — Thirteen team-statistic pairs were measured directly on all 30 teams from the official advanced table. Net rating explains 87% of the variation in win percentage; pace is nearly unrelated to net rating (r = -0.34, p = 0.063) and assist percentage is unrelated to offensive efficiency (r = 0.02, p = 0.92). The parlay and same-game-parlay correlations that drive pricing are model-derived and labelled as such.
    The distinction matters most for parlays, because pricing correlated legs as independent is the classic way to overpay.
  8. The word 'edge' on this site does not mean edge against a sportsbook — No sportsbook price is published by an NBA-operated source, so no official link can be attached to one. The site bets the originated line against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. Until real prices are logged, 'units won' means model-vs-naive-model units.
    This is the single most important caveat for anyone reading the leaderboard.
  9. The 2026-11-13 slate is settled, and it did not go well - which is the system working — Every published paper pick on the 2026-11-13 slate was settled against the official finals captured on 2026-11-14. The seven gated singles went 3-4 (-1.40 units at -110) and all three two-leg parlays lost. The picks were pre-registered: the model blocks in the live file are identical to the copy frozen at 2026-11-13T22:45Z, before tip. The originated margin line was off by 12.39 points on average across the nine games - consistent with the 12.98 MAE on the wider window, and a useful reminder that a night like this is normal at this sample size.
    Two gaps closed the same morning: the finals now exist (F13 resolved) and the 18 stale team records were refreshed from the official cards (F14 resolved). Two remain open and are published as such: at the newest injury edition five of the ten teams playing on 11/14 have still not filed, and no edition lists any player for the 11/16 and 11/17 games.
  10. The 60-game extension is real, but it cannot be reproduced from this repository alone — The 2026-11-13 finals are captured, transcribed three independent times that all agree 9/9, and used to settle every published 11/13 pick (seven singles 3-4 at -1.40 units, three two-leg parlays 0-3). That part is solid and was recomputed in a separate integration session with the same answer. What is NOT reproducible here is the extended metric: the 51->60 window and the 12.98 vs 13.62 margin MAE depend on a team-ratings refresh, and https://www.nba.com/stats/teams/advanced?Season=2026-27&SeasonType=Regular+Season returns no data table to this environment (flag F24, re-tested 2026-11-14). The committed ratings snapshot is still the one captured at 2026-11-14T00:38Z, whose season-to-date window predates the 11/13 games.
    A reader can check every score and every settlement row against NBA.com, and should. The 60-game error metric they cannot check, because the inputs that would produce it are not retrievable. Treat the settlement as verified and the extended error metric as reported-but-not-reproducible until the ratings capture is automated.
Two models, two different lines - which one is authoritative?

Every game below carries a line from the validated model (engine/pipeline.py, the one tested on 60 out-of-sample games). A second, independent line-origination model (RecordLine-1, scripts/originate_horizon.py) produces its own spread for the same games. They are different models, not two renderings of one number: on the 17 games they share they differ on 17, and on 2 of those the two disagree about which team is the favourite (largest gap 11.8 points).

The line shown on this page is the authoritative one because it is the only one whose error has been measured out of sample. Do not average the two and do not treat the second as a second opinion on the same estimate - it is a different, unmeasured model. The full row-by-row comparison is in Official Source Check, section 5. Choosing between them, or retiring one, is an open decision (flag F26, suggestion SG15).

SIGN CONVENTION (read this once): 'Model spread' is the projected home margin, i.e. home points minus away points. A NEGATIVE number means the home team is projected to LOSE by that many points. That is the opposite of the way a book prints a home spread, and the two must not be mixed up (flag F27). Spread and total are the originated lines (model). Edge is the model minus the naive reference line built from the same official ratings; positive spread edge means the model likes the home side more than the reference does. Rest flags a back-to-back. Every row links to the official NBA.com game page.
