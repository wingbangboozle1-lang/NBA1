What this site is, and what it is not

This is an origination and research workspace. It produces its own lines from official NBA data and then tries hard to prove that those lines carry skill. As of this build no strategy has cleared the skill gate: the best one is 16-7 at Tier 3 - Quarter unit, which is inside what luck alone produces. Every strategy is therefore capped at a quarter unit or held back as research-only, and that is the result, not an obstacle to be worked around.

No sportsbook price is used anywhere, because no NBA-operated source publishes one. “Edge” on this site means the model against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. It does not mean an edge against a book.

Executive summary

The horizon now runs the full three days: 27 games from 14 to 17 November 2026, with an originated line, a paper pick and an injury read-out for every one, and a source link on every row. The newest official injury-report edition (11/13/26 07:30 PM ET) has been captured and transcribed row by row. The out-of-sample window is 51 games, and the honest read on it is unchanged: a small edge on the margin, no edge on totals or winners, and one strategy whose good record is still inside what luck produces.

Ten strategies were written, seven validation gates were defined, and all ten were run against a strictly out-of-sample window: all 51 completed games from 2026-11-06 to 2026-11-12, every one predicted using team ratings computed only from 2026-10-20 to 2026-11-05. No game in the test window contributes to the ratings that predict it. On top of that the system now originates a line for every game in the next three days - 14, 15, 16 and 17 November - from official ratings and the newest official injury report.

Verdict — PROVISIONAL - not funded
The model beats the naive reference on margin accuracy and loses to it on totals and on picking winners. The best strategy is 16-7, worth +7.55 model units - but with an exact p-value of 0.093 and a Wilson lower bound of 0.491 against a 0.524 break-even, it fails the skill gate. Every strategy stays at quarter-unit or research-only, and the 27 new lines are published as lines and paper picks, not as recommendations.
Why: Fifty-one games with a 17.4-point residual standard deviation narrows the uncertainty a lot but does not remove it. On 23 graded plays, a 16-7 run happens often enough by chance that it cannot be called skill.
  1. The model wins on the margin and loses on totals and winners — Margin: MAE 12.89 against 13.19 for the naive reference, RMSE 17.10 against 17.70 - the model is better on both. Totals: MAE 14.86 against 14.75, RMSE 19.88 against 19.59 - the reference is better on both. Straight up both went 37-14, but the reference has the better Brier score (0.1889 against 0.1970) and the better log loss (0.5567 against 0.5768).
    The refinements buy a real but tiny margin improvement and nothing else. The probability calibration is the weakest part of the model, and it is where the remaining work should go.
  2. Growing the window from 28 games to 51 changed a conclusion - which is the most useful thing that happened — On the 28-game window the model beat the reference on totals (MAE 14.50 against 14.75). On 51 games it does not (14.86 against 14.75). The sign flipped. That is exactly the failure mode the sample gate exists to catch, and it is why nothing here is being promoted on the earlier reading.
    Any single-window result at this sample size should be treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.
  3. The best strategy is 16-7, and that is not enough — S1 (side edge vs the reference spread) produced 23 graded plays at 16-7, worth +7.55 model units and a 32.8% return at -110. Its exact binomial p-value against a coin flip is 0.093. The Wilson 95% interval on its hit rate is 0.491 to 0.844, and the lower bound is below the 0.5238 break-even, so the skill gate fails. S5 (back-to-back fade) is 8-4 on 12 plays, also short.
    A 69.6% hit rate on 23 plays is a promising signal and nothing more. The leaderboard caps it at quarter-unit regardless of its score, which is the designed behaviour.
  4. Home court looks normal; the back-to-back penalty is large but uncertain — Home-court advantage estimates at +2.79 points with a standard error of 2.48 (t = 1.12), squarely in the historical NBA range. The back-to-back penalty estimates at -6.10 points with a standard error of 4.87 (t = -1.25); because |t| >= 1 the model applies the full 6.10, but the standard error is most of the estimate and detection is still incomplete for 2026-11-06, which biases it toward zero.
    Both are published with their standard errors and both are fitted on the test window, which is disclosed on every row they touch.
  5. Shrinkage was derived rather than guessed, and the curve is flat where it matters — The empirical-Bayes calculation gives k = 2.9, weighting each team's observed net rating at 0.739. The fixed-k sweep shows margin MAE moving only from 13.28 at k = 0 to 12.91 at k = 4, then degrading to 13.65 by k = 20. Heavy shrinkage is clearly wrong; the choice between light and none barely matters.
    The constant is defensible, its sensitivity is published, and it is not a source of hidden risk.
  6. Availability impact is a measured floor, not an estimate — Minutes are only known for the 50 players in the captured official table. Players outside it contribute 0.00 to the impact, so teams missing rotation players who are not top-50 scorers show a smaller number than the truth - sometimes zero. The minutes-coverage column sits next to every figure so a 0.00 is never read as 'healthy'.
    Correct but incomplete - the Injuries page says so on every row rather than hiding it.
  7. Correlation is measured where it can be and flagged where it cannot — Thirteen team-statistic pairs were measured directly on all 30 teams from the official advanced table. Net rating explains 87% of the variation in win percentage; pace is nearly unrelated to net rating (r = -0.34, p = 0.063) and assist percentage is unrelated to offensive efficiency (r = 0.02, p = 0.92). The parlay and same-game-parlay correlations that drive pricing are model-derived and labelled as such.
    The distinction matters most for parlays, because pricing correlated legs as independent is the classic way to overpay.
  8. The word 'edge' on this site does not mean edge against a sportsbook — No sportsbook price is published by an NBA-operated source, so no official link can be attached to one. The site bets the originated line against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. Until real prices are logged, 'units won' means model-vs-naive-model units.
    This is the single most important caveat for anyone reading the leaderboard.
  9. The three-day horizon is now live, and it is the part that is hardest to keep honest — 27 games from 2026-11-14 to 2026-11-17, each with an originated spread, total and fair moneyline, a gated paper pick, rest and back-to-back flags, and an injury read-out. The schedule was read from the official NBA.com Schedule page and independently cross-checked against another session's capture of the same page: 27 of 27 rows agree, 0 mismatches. The injury report was taken from the 07:30 PM ET edition of the official PDF, which is newer than the 05:15 PM edition this repository previously held.
    What is still not knowable is published as such rather than filled in. The ratings window ends at 11/12: the nine 11/13 games are Final and were used to settle the 11/13 lines and to refresh every stale team record on these rows (0 mismatches remaining, 2026-11-14), but the ratings themselves have not been re-fitted, so every line still rests on ratings one night short. And at the 07:30 PM edition five of the ten teams playing on 11/14 had not filed an injury report, while no edition yet covers the 11/15 to 11/17 games. Those lines carry no injury adjustment and say so.
Two models, two different lines - which one is authoritative?

Every game below carries a line from the validated model (engine/pipeline.py, the one tested on 51 out-of-sample games). A second, independent line-origination model (RecordLine-1, scripts/originate_horizon.py) produces its own spread for the same games. They are different models, not two renderings of one number: on the 17 games they share they differ on 17, and on 2 of those the two disagree about which team is the favourite (largest gap 11.8 points).

The line shown on this page is the authoritative one because it is the only one whose error has been measured out of sample. Do not average the two and do not treat the second as a second opinion on the same estimate - it is a different, unmeasured model. The full row-by-row comparison is in Official Source Check, section 5. Choosing between them, or retiring one, is an open decision (flag F26, suggestion SG15).

SIGN CONVENTION (read this once): 'Model spread' is the projected home margin, i.e. home points minus away points. A NEGATIVE number means the home team is projected to LOSE by that many points. That is the opposite of the way a book prints a home spread, and the two must not be mixed up (flag F27). Spread and total are the originated lines (model). Edge is the model minus the naive reference line built from the same official ratings; positive spread edge means the model likes the home side more than the reference does. Rest flags a back-to-back. Every row links to the official NBA.com game page.
