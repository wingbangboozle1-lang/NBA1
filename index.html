What this site is, and what it is not

This is an origination and research workspace. It produces its own lines from official NBA data and then tries hard to prove that those lines carry skill. As of this build no strategy is funded. The best one — 23-9 at Tier 3 - Quarter unit — has cleared the registered luck gate G4 (Wilson 95% lower bound on its hit rate above the 52.38% break-even; the exact binomial p = 0.020 is uncorrected for the number of strategies tried, and this gate set registers no multiplicity gate), but it fails the calibration and stability gates and the availability gate cannot run, so it stays capped at a quarter unit. Every strategy is therefore capped at a quarter unit or held back as research-only, and that is the result, not an obstacle to be worked around.

No sportsbook price is used anywhere, because no NBA-operated source publishes one. “Edge” on this site means the model against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. It does not mean an edge against a book.

Executive summary

The rolling window is 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-18: 34 games on the official schedule, 28 of which had not tipped at publication. Behind them sit 190 official finals, re-read line by line from NBA.com box scores.

This session re-cut the model inputs from first principles. The three official team-stat pages render their tables in the browser and carry no data in an HTTP response, so possessions, offensive and defensive rating and pace were rebuilt out of official box scores for all 190 completed games of the season instead - every number traceable to an official game page. Home court is now pinned to the measured home-win rate (0.5263 over 190 finals, 1.108 points) rather than fitted. Two genuinely independent estimators are published with their disagreement as the headline; they disagree by 4.05 points on the spread on average. The 2026-11-15 slate is partly settled from official finals: two games were Final and both published tickets won, the other seven are PENDING.

Verdict — PROVISIONAL - not funded
The model is now built entirely on values read from official pages, but no strategy has passed the validation gates and no market price exists anywhere in the repository, so nothing here is a betting recommendation.
Why: Every gate that can run has been run and none has passed. The gates that cannot run - the two that need a closing price - are blocked on data nobody in this project has. Publishing the block rather than the number is the whole point.
  1. Home court is measured, not fitted: 0.5263 over 190 official finals — Home teams won 100 of 190 official finals from 2026-10-20 to 2026-11-15. Converted through the empirical margin distribution (sigma 16.78) that is 1.108 points, and that is the number every line now uses. An earlier build fitted home court freely and got 3.51 points with a standard error of 2.28 - a number the data cannot support.
    The pinned value is smaller and honest. It is not a tweak; it removes a free parameter that was soaking up noise.
  2. The two estimators disagree by 4.05 points on the spread on average — Estimator 1 builds the line from possessions and points per possession. Estimator 2 uses a completely different information set: a least-squares power rating solved from official final margins, and the teams' own scored and allowed rates. Across the 34 games on the slate they disagree by 4.05 points on the spread on average and up to 8.8.
    The disagreement is the headline number on the projection page. The 50/50 blend sits underneath it and is never the headline.
  3. Two independent estimators are only useful if they are actually independent — The first attempt at a second estimator reused the same efficiency ratings with different arithmetic and disagreed by 0.1 to 0.2 points - noise dressed up as a signal. It was replaced with a margin-based power rating on a different information set.
    A disagreement that is smaller than the rounding is not a confidence signal. This is worth checking on any future estimator added to the site.
  4. The official injury report changed 28 times in one day — The league published 75 editions on 2026-11-15. Every one was downloaded, hashed, stored and parsed, and each was diffed against the edition immediately before it. Twenty-eight status changes were found, including T.J. McConnell Probable to Available at 12:15 PM ET and Andrew Nembhard Questionable to Out in the same minute.
    Any rotation read older than a few hours is stale. The banner on the Injury PDF Ledger page prints the revision time and the retrieval time so the reader can see exactly how old it is.
  5. Travel is computed and flagged, not quietly used — Arena names, cities and states are official - they are printed on the box score of a game played there. Latitude and longitude are not published by any NBA page, so the great-circle distances are derived and flagged (F94). No travel value moves a line.
    Rest, back-to-back and 3-in-4 are computed from the official schedule and are solid. Travel is a candidate input awaiting a source.

No closing line exists anywhere in this repository and no NBA-operated source publishes one, so the weight is 0 and no blended price is published (F95).
    This is the single change that would unlock the most: two validation gates and the prior are all blocked on the same missing input.

  13. The model wins on the margin and loses on totals and winners — Margin: MAE 12.98 against 13.62 for the naive reference, RMSE 17.12 against 17.88 - the model is better on both. Totals: MAE 14.83 against 14.72, RMSE 19.35 against 19.10 - the reference is better on both. Straight up the reference went 42-18 and the model 40-20, and the reference has the better Brier score (0.2001 against 0.2020) and the better log loss (0.5827 against 0.5873).
    The refinements buy a real but tiny margin improvement and nothing else. The probability calibration is the weakest part of the model, and it is where the remaining work should go.
  14. Growing the window keeps changing what can be claimed - which is the point of the sample gate — On the 28-game window the model beat the reference on totals (MAE 14.50 against 14.75). On 51 games it did not (14.86 against 14.75) - the sign flipped. On the 60-game window the sign holds (14.83 against 14.72) and the gap narrowed, but the conclusion stands. Each addition of data has moved at least one claim.
    Any single-window result at this sample size should be treated as a hypothesis, not a finding.
  15. S1 has cleared the luck gate at 23-9 - and failed two others, which is the honest headline — S1 (side edge vs the reference spread) produced 32 graded plays at 23-9, worth +11.91 model units and a 37.2% return at -110. Its exact binomial p-value against a coin flip is 0.020 and the Wilson 95% interval on its hit rate is 0.546 to 0.844 - the lower bound is now above the 0.5238 break-even, so G4 (skill, not luck) passes for the first time. But its hit rate of 0.719 against a mean predicted probability of 0.627 fails the calibration gate (gap 0.091 against a 0.08 tolerance), and the stability gate fails because the bootstrap interval on the model's margin MAE (10.2 to 15.94) still contains the reference MAE (13.62). The availability gate cannot run at all for the graded window.
    This is what selectively skill-based looks like: one gate says this is probably not luck, two gates say the model does not understand why, and the funding tier stays at quarter unit until every gate that can be run has been run and passed.
  16. Home court looks normal; the back-to-back penalty is large but uncertain — Home-court advantage estimates at +3.51 points with a standard error of 2.28 (t = 1.54) across 60 games, squarely in the historical NBA range. The back-to-back penalty estimates at -6.26 points with a standard error of 4.86 (t = -1.29); because |t| >= 1 the model applies the full 6.26, but the standard error is most of the estimate.
    Both are published with their standard errors and both are fitted on the test window, which is disclosed on every row they touch.
  17. Shrinkage was derived rather than guessed, and the curve is flat where it matters — The empirical-Bayes calculation gives k = 2.9, weighting each team's observed net rating at 0.739. The fixed-k sweep shows margin MAE moving from 13.62 at k = 0 to 12.91 at k = 4, then degrading to 13.36 by k = 20. Heavy shrinkage is clearly wrong; the choice between light and none barely matters.
    The constant is defensible, its sensitivity is published, and it is not a source of hidden risk.
  18. Availability impact is a measured floor, not an estimate — Minutes are only known for the 50 players in the captured official table. Players outside it contribute 0.00 to the impact, so teams missing rotation players who are not top-50 scorers show a smaller number than the truth - sometimes zero. The minutes-coverage column sits next to every figure so a 0.00 is never read as 'healthy'.
    Correct but incomplete - the Injuries page says so on every row rather than hiding it.
  19. Correlation is measured where it can be and flagged where it cannot — Thirteen team-statistic pairs were measured directly on all 30 teams from the official advanced table. Net rating explains 87% of the variation in win percentage; pace is nearly unrelated to net rating (r = -0.34, p = 0.063) and assist percentage is unrelated to offensive efficiency (r = 0.02, p = 0.92). The parlay and same-game-parlay correlations that drive pricing are model-derived and labelled as such.
    The distinction matters most for parlays, because pricing correlated legs as independent is the classic way to overpay.
  20. The word 'edge' on this site does not mean edge against a sportsbook — No sportsbook price is published by an NBA-operated source, so no official link can be attached to one. The site bets the originated line against a deliberately naive reference model built from the same official ratings. Until real prices are logged, 'units won' means model-vs-naive-model units.
    This is the single most important caveat for anyone reading the leaderboard.
  21. The 2026-11-13 slate is settled, and it did not go well - which is the system working — Every published paper pick on the 2026-11-13 slate was settled against the official finals captured on 2026-11-14. The seven gated singles went 3-4 (-1.40 units at -110) and all three two-leg parlays lost. The picks were pre-registered: the model blocks in the live file are identical to the copy frozen at 2026-11-13T22:45Z, before tip. The originated margin line was off by 12.39 points on average across the nine games - consistent with the 12.98 MAE on the wider window, and a useful reminder that a night like this is normal at this sample size.
    Two gaps closed the same morning: the finals now exist (F13 resolved) and the 18 stale team records were refreshed from the official cards (F14 resolved). Two remain open and are published as such: at the newest injury edition five of the ten teams playing on 11/14 have still not filed, and no edition lists any player for the 11/16 and 11/17 games.

Two models, two different lines - which one is authoritative?

Every game below carries a line from the validated model (engine/pipeline.py, the one tested on 60 out-of-sample games). A second, independent line-origination model (RecordLine-1, scripts/originate_horizon.py) produces its own spread for the same games. They are different models, not two renderings of one number: on the 17 games they share they differ on 17, and on 2 of those the two disagree about which team is the favourite (largest gap 11.8 points).

The line shown on this page is the authoritative one because it is the only one whose error has been measured out of sample. Do not average the two and do not treat the second as a second opinion on the same estimate - it is a different, unmeasured model. The full row-by-row comparison is in Official Source Check, section 5. Choosing between them, or retiring one, is an open decision (flag F26, suggestion SG15).

SIGN CONVENTION (read this once): 'Model spread' is the projected home margin, i.e. home points minus away points. A NEGATIVE number means the home team is projected to LOSE by that many points. That is the opposite of the way a book prints a home spread, and the two must not be mixed up (flag F27). Spread and total are the originated lines (model). Edge is the model minus the naive reference line built from the same official ratings; positive spread edge means the model likes the home side more than the reference does. Rest flags a back-to-back. Every row links to the official NBA.com game page.
