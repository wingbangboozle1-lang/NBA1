Method

Exactly how each number is produced, and what each one is allowed to claim.

No strategy is funded. All 12 flagged items and every unvalidated number are published here. There is no market odds feed, so nothing on this site is a claim of real-world profit.

Inputs

One input only: the official final scores and schedule published by NBA.com. Every figure on this site derives from 151 verified scores. There is no odds feed, no possession data, no play-by-play and no player game log, because none is published by an official source that can be read here.

The line

  1. Margin. Simple Rating System: rating = average margin + average opponent rating, solved by iteration, shrunk toward zero with 4 pseudo-games. Predicted home margin = rating difference + home-court (2.21 points, estimated from the data; zero for neutral-site games).
  2. Total. Opponent-adjusted points scored and points allowed, solved by iteration, shrunk toward the league mean with 6 pseudo-games. Predicted total = home offence + away defence − league mean, plus the mirror image, plus the home scoring edge.
  3. Probability. Normal CDF around the point prediction with the out-of-sample residual standard deviation: 15.87 points for margin, 19.27 for total. These are measured out of sample, not in sample. See F10 for the one caveat.
  4. Price. American odds = the reciprocal of the probability. No margin is added — this desk originates lines, it does not make a book.

How a strategy can beat the line

A bettor using the same information as the line cannot beat it, by construction. So each strategy is required to add one input the line does not use — rest days, back-to-back status or schedule density, all read from consecutive official schedule dates. Each bet is then struck at the line’s own fair price (1 / pbaseline) and settled there. A strategy profits only if its extra input genuinely moves the probability in a direction reality confirms.

Each strategy is run at three trigger levels (0.02, 0.03, 0.04 probability points) and every cell is published. The trigger is not tuned to the results.

Luck removal

Each cell’s ROI is compared against 5,000 Monte-Carlo simulations of a no-skill bettor placing the identical bet list at the identical prices. Three things come out of it: the null median (the luck baseline), the 95th percentile (the luck line), and pnull (how often pure luck did at least this well). A separate power calculation reports the smallest true ROI this sample could detect at all.
