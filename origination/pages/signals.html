Signal study

Does the signal carry information the line missed? Tested on all out-of-sample games.

No strategy is funded. All 12 flagged items and every unvalidated number are published here. There is no market odds feed, so nothing on this site is a claim of real-world profit.

Testing the signal, not the bet record

A bet-level test can only use the handful of games that clear a trigger — on this window that is 6 to 25 games, far too few to conclude anything. So the signal itself is tested on every evaluated game instead: regress the model’s out-of-sample error on the signal.

  • A slope reliably away from zero means the signal contains information the line missed.
  • A slope near zero means it does not, and no amount of extra data will help.
  • For the two rows labelled “slope 1 = correctly sized”, a slope of 1.0 would mean the strategy’s weights are right as well as real. A slope of 0 means the shift adds nothing; a slope of 2 means it should be doubled.

Reading

  • Rest days is the only live candidate. Each extra day of rest a team holds over its opponent is worth +2.82 points of margin in this sample. It is not statistically significant (p = 0.130) but it is the only signal here that is not sitting on zero, and it has a concrete resolution point at about 340 games.
  • Back-to-back alone is weaker than it looks. The raw differential is +2.88 points but with p = 0.398 — mostly because it is a noisier version of the rest variable.
  • Schedule density is nothing here. +0.51 points per extra game in four days, p = 0.849.
  • The totals market shows no fatigue effect at all. Each team on the second night of a back-to-back moves the total by +0.035 points, p = 0.991. If there is an edge in totals it is not here.
