Removing luck

A bettor taking n even-money bets at a fair price has an ROI distribution centred on zero with a spread that shrinks only as 1/√n. At 11 bets that spread is enormous. So instead of asking “did it win?”, this page asks the only question that matters:

Given this exact bet list, how often would a bettor with no skill at all have done at least this well?

Every row below answers it with 5,000 Monte-Carlo simulations of a no-skill bettor placing the identical bets, at the same prices, in the same order. The 95th percentile of that distribution is the luck line.

Worked example: the current leader

S10@0.02 — Adaptive Rest Model (spread) went 7–4 on 11 bets, an ROI of 27.3%. Read alone that looks like a winner. Read against its own null it does not:

  • A no-skill bettor placing those same 11 bets has a median ROI of 9.1%.
  • Five per cent of no-skill bettors beat 45.5% ROI purely by chance.
  • This strategy returned 27.3% — -18.2 percentage points against the luck line.
  • 2,644.0% of no-skill bettors matched or beat it.
  • The smallest true ROI this sample could even detect at 80% power is 85.2%.

The strategy is not wrong. It is simply unproven, and at 11 bets it could not be otherwise.
