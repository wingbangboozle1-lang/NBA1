Player props

What is missing, why it is missing, and what it would take to unblock.

No strategy is funded. All 12 flagged items and every unvalidated number are published here. There is no market odds feed, so nothing on this site is a claim of real-world profit.

Status: blocked, not started

Player props are the largest part of the modern betting menu and the entire basis of same-game-parlay demand. They are also the one part of this brief that cannot be attempted honestly with the data available.

A season average is not a game log. Originating a player-prop line needs the game-by-game distribution behind the average, which no official page reachable here publishes. Rotation minutes for a replacement player are therefore not estimated anywhere on this site.

What originating a prop line actually requires

  1. A game-by-game log for that player. A prop line is a point on a distribution, so the distribution is the input. A season average of 27.8 points tells you nothing about the shape — and the shape is what decides whether over 25.5 is a good bet.
  2. Minutes, with the rotation context. A player averaging 32 minutes in games he starts is a different proposition the night a teammate returns.
  3. The opponent’s scheme and pace. Rebounds and assists are strongly opponent-dependent.
  4. Correlations between the legs of a combo. Points and rebounds for the same player are positively correlated; points and assists for two players on the same team often are not. Pricing a combo at independence would be wrong, and by how much is exactly what needs measuring.

None of the four is available from an official source here. Item 4 is partly available for team outcomes — see the correlations page — but not for players.

What is available

Two partial official leaderboards, 24 players by minutes and 50 by scoring, listed in full on the injuries page. They are enough to size the production at risk for 8 of the 117 players in the latest injury report. They are not enough to originate a single prop line.

What would unblock it

A game-by-game box-score feed. One exists for money; the NBA stats API would also serve if it were reachable from this environment, which it is not. Until one is licensed this workstream should be formally closed rather than left open — see Q3.
