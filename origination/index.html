Executive summary

This desk originates its own NBA lines from one input: the official final scores published by NBA.com. It then runs a leaderboard competition in which every strategy must beat that originated line using information the line itself does not use. Nothing is funded yet, and the reason is quantified rather than asserted.

1. The game log is complete and verified.

All 30 teams’ game counts reconstructed from the 151 captured scores plus the 23 unpublished fixtures equal the official standings exactly (174 games). This is the strongest available evidence that the log is complete and correctly oriented.

2. The line-generating model beats a naive baseline.

Out-of-sample margin error 12.43 points against 14.26 for a naive constant, Brier 0.209 against 0.249, and 68-33 straight up (67.3%). The line is a credible baseline to test strategies against.

3. No strategy passes the gates, and the reason is sample size, not design.

Only 4 of 39 cells reach the 25-bet floor. The best one shows 27.3% ROI on 11 bets, but a no-skill bettor beats 45.5% ROI 5% of the time at that sample. Observed performance is inside the luck band, not outside it.

4. One signal is worth keeping; three are dead.

Rest-day differential moves the margin by +2.82 points per day (t = +1.53, p = 0.130, n = 101) — not significant, but the only candidate that is not obviously zero, and it would need about 340 games to resolve. Back-to-back, schedule density and every totals signal are indistinguishable from nothing.

5. Correlations are close to zero, which is itself the answer.

Margin and total correlate at r = -0.030 over 151 games (p = 0.72), and the out-of-sample residual correlation is r = +0.018. Same-game parlays should be priced at independence until a much larger sample says otherwise.

6. Injuries are published as context, not baked into the number.

117 players are listed in the latest official report; only 8 of them have a verified season average in the partial leaderboards available. Rotation minutes are not estimated anywhere.

What would change the answer

  1. More games. The season adds roughly seven games a day. The rest signal needs about 340 games; there are 151 today.
  2. A market price. Without it, “edge” only ever means “beats our own number”.
  3. A player game log. It is the only thing standing between this desk and player props.
