Luck and skill on the official record

Arithmetic on 160 official finals: how many games were actually in doubt, which teams are winning games their point differential does not support, and why that decides what a hit rate is worth.

The question this page answers
How much of the win-loss record is luck? The answer here is arithmetic on official final scores only: points for, points against, wins, and a Pythagorean expectation. It describes variance. It does not forecast, it does not adjust for opponent strength, rest or injury, and it is not a model.

The record is 23 games short, and that is measured, not estimated
Recomputing every team's record from the official game log gives 320 team-games against the standings' 366. The gap is uniform: 14 teams are short exactly 1 game, 16 exactly 2, and none is short 0 or 3 or more. Because the shortfall is the same for everyone, team-to-team comparisons on this page survive it. A full-season record does not, and the page says so rather than rounding it away. WHICH games are missing. The official day pages for 2026-10-24 and 2026-11-02 print no result rows (flag F1), so the missing fixtures cannot be named from an official source. They are therefore NOT named, NOT inferred from the standings residual, and NOT scored.

Live lines versus out-of-sample lines: two different questions

The site carries two line sets. They are both true and they may not be added together, so the difference is published as a number instead of being left for a reader to trip over.

Line set Games Mean |model − reference| Median Largest What it may be used for Source
Out-of-sample walk-forward 60 3.71 3.4 9.3 the only window a skill claim may be made on: every prediction was fitted on earlier games walk-forward rows
Live horizon (unplayed) 27 3.76 3.2 10.5 pricing the next three days only: no outcome exists yet, so it cannot support a performance claim live horizon rows

Anything about skill. The live horizon rows carry ratings that may have seen results the out-of-sample rows never saw, and none of the live games has been played yet, so there is no outcome to grade against. A larger gap on unplayed games is not an edge and is not published as one.

Live ratings now include the 11/13 finals, so a live row may carry a rating that has seen a result the out-of-sample rows never saw. That is why the two gaps above are close in size and still not comparable: one set is scored, the other is not. The reconciliation is generated by scripts/s14x_crosscheck.py and published as site/data/s14x-crosscheck.json.

Why this matters for the strategies

A strategy's record is only meaningful against the number of games that were actually in doubt.

On this sample 13 of 160 games (8.1%) were decided by 3 or fewer points, so a hit rate is being earned mostly in games that were not coin flips. That is the opposite of the usual intuition and it cuts both ways: a strategy that wins close games is beating luck, and a strategy that wins blowouts is riding a team quality the ratings already know about. The site reports both, and the gates are what decide which one a result is.
