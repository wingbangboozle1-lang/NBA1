Two independent estimators — their disagreement is the headline

Every game below carries a line from two models built from the same official inputs by different code: estimator A is scripts/originate_horizon.py (this page), estimator B is engine/pipeline.py (the out-of-sample-validated build). The disagreement column is the first number shown, and the two spreads sit underneath it — not a blend, because averaging two models whose errors have not been measured against each other would hide the one signal we can read.

On the 13 games both models cover (they overlap on 2026-11-16, 2026-11-17), the mean absolute disagreement is 4.69 points and the largest is 12.6; on 1 of them the two disagree about which team is the favourite. Note the vintage difference: estimator B's ratings window is its own published cut, estimator A's is the re-cut above, so some of the gap is input age, not model opinion. Choosing between them is open decision F26.

Where most of the gap comes from. The mean signed difference (A − B) is -3.89 points, i.e. B favours the home team by that much. B's line is built with a fitted home-court advantage of +3.508 points (t = 1.539, estimated in-sample on the test window) and a back-to-back penalty of 6.262 points; estimator A applies neither, because no official page publishes either number. The fitted HCA alone accounts for 90% of the mean gap. That is the whole of requirement R08 in one number: the measured home-win rate is 0.5455 (36 of 66 official finals), and a fitted +3.51-point edge is a far stronger claim than that evidence supports.

What is published instead of a fake number is the exact Fréchet–Hoeffding band the joint probability must lie in for any rho, and the naive product is the price.
