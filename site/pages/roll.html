Two independent estimators — their disagreement is the headline

Every game below carries a line from two models built from the same official inputs by different code: estimator A is scripts/originate_horizon.py (this page), estimator B is engine/pipeline.py (the out-of-sample-validated build). The disagreement column is the first number shown, and the two spreads sit underneath it — not a blend, because averaging two models whose errors have not been measured against each other would hide the one signal we can read.

On the 29 games both models cover (they overlap on 2026-11-16, 2026-11-17, 2026-11-18, 2026-11-19), the mean absolute disagreement is 4.40 points and the largest is 12.6; on 2 of them the two disagree about which team is the favourite. Note the vintage difference: estimator B's ratings window is its own published cut, estimator A's is the re-cut above, so some of the gap is input age, not model opinion. Choosing between them is open decision F26.

Where most of the gap comes from. The mean signed difference (A − B) is -3.49 points, i.e. B favours the home team by that much. B's line is built with a fitted home-court advantage of +3.508 points (t = 1.539, estimated in-sample on the test window) and a back-to-back penalty of 6.262 points; estimator A applies neither, because no official page publishes either number. The fitted HCA alone accounts for 100% of the mean gap. That is the whole of requirement R08 in one number: the measured home-win rate is 0.5135 (38 of 74 official finals), and a fitted +3.51-point edge is a far stronger claim than that evidence supports.

4. Same-game side-and-total — priced with the measured rho

Leg 1: the home team wins. Leg 2: the game total beats the originated total. These two legs come from the same final score, so their correlation is directly observable and is measured from 74 official finals (2026-11-06 to 2026-11-15): ρ(home margin, game total) = -0.105 (p = 0.3704), and -0.1112 on regulation games only. Both are statistically indistinguishable from zero, which is itself the finding: the naive product is very nearly the right price for this pair. The measured rho price and the naive product are printed side by side on every row (R14); a second register (rho_2026_27.json) measures the same pair at -0.0199 over a longer 190-game window — the register conflict is flagged F128. P(total > total) is exactly 0.500 because the originated total is this model's own median — stated rather than dressed up as a model edge.
