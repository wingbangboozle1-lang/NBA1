Two independent estimators — their disagreement is the headline

Every game below carries a line from two models built from the same official inputs by different code: estimator A is scripts/originate_horizon.py (this page), estimator B is engine/pipeline.py (the out-of-sample-validated build). The disagreement column is the first number shown, and the two spreads sit underneath it — not a blend, because averaging two models whose errors have not been measured against each other would hide the one signal we can read.

On the 4 games both models cover (they overlap on 2026-11-19), the mean absolute disagreement is 4.28 points and the largest is 9.0; on 1 of them the two disagree about which team is the favourite. Note the vintage difference: estimator B's ratings window is its own published cut, estimator A's is the re-cut above, so some of the gap is input age, not model opinion. Choosing between them is open decision F26.

Where most of the gap comes from. The mean signed difference (A − B) is +3.68 points, A favours the home side by that much, i.e. B's home-side edge is now the smaller of the two. B's line is built with a home-court advantage pinned to the measured home-win rate, +0.59 points (rate 0.5135 over 74 official completed games, sigma_margin 17.409312; the fitted value +3.508 points, t = 1.539, is recorded as a diagnostic and not applied - requirement R09) and a back-to-back penalty of 6.262 points; estimator A applies neither, because no official page publishes either number. The applied home-court term accounts for 16% of the mean gap. Home court is now the smaller of the two claims: pinning it removed 2.92 points of B's home-side edge, which is why the mean signed gap moved when the pin was wired in.
