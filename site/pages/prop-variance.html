Why this page exists

Until now every prop row on this site published a projection with the probability column left blank, because game-to-game variance had never been measured from official data (open question Q3, flag F2). Without a dispersion figure a projection is a point with no width, so no prop could be sized, no prop combination could be priced, and no prop could be graded against anything.

This page measures that width from the official NBA.com game-log pages for the 2026-11-14 slate cohort, states the sampling limit next to every number, and reports what it does and does not license. It prices 10 of the 88 horizon prop rows; the other 78 still carry no probability, exactly as before.

4. Which anchor forecasts a player better

The question the whole props module depends on: should a projection start from the season average, from recent form, or from the last game?

PRA — 9.82 (30 rows) 8.35 (20 rows) 7.01 (30 rows) see the note —

Read this carefully. All three forecasts are scored on the same captured games. The season average is contaminated - those games are inside it - and it sees 10-12 games while the leave-one-out form mean sees three. So the honest result is narrow: on this sample, throwing away season data for a three-game window makes the forecast worse, and chasing the single previous game makes it worse still (points MAE 6.53 vs 8.02 vs 9.97). It is not evidence that the season average is the best possible anchor, and it is not evidence that a properly weighted blend would lose - that test needs a longer logged window, which is what the remaining capture would buy.

Deviations are taken from each player's own mean before pooling, so a starter's level cannot masquerade as a minutes effect. Beta is a pooled within-player regression of relative production on relative minutes: a 10% cut in minutes predicts a 12.9% cut in points and a 9.3% cut in PRA, while assists barely move with minutes at all. That matters for the injury module, which currently applies a declared production-transfer rule of 0.55 with no measured figure behind it (see its model_assumptions): points transfer at roughly one-for-one with minutes, rebounds and assists do not, so a flat 0.55 is too small for a scorer and too large for a passer. It is a fitted sample of 40 rows from 10 players, not a league fit.
