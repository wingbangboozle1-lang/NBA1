P1 — blocks a decision being made now

q-01 · Is this project trying to beat a market, or to measure model quality? P1

What we know
No licensed odds feed is connected and no NBA-operated price exists. Every edge on the site is model-vs-naive-model at a -110 convention chosen by the repository.
What is missing
A decision, plus — if the answer is 'beat a market' — a dated, licensed closing-price source per game.
Why it decides
It changes what 'validated' means. Against a market, S1 must beat a real price and pay real vig. Against a naive model, S1 is already mildly ahead on margin (MAE 12.98 vs 13.62) and behind on totals (14.83 vs 14.72).
How to find out
Either (a) buy a licensed historical + closing-line feed and backfill the 60-game window, or (b) publish one sentence on the landing page: 'this project measures model quality; no market edge is claimed or measurable'. Option (b) costs nothing and can be done today.

q-02 · Do NBA.com's terms permit publishing derived lines and a paid reward structure? P1

What we know
The site publishes derived numbers built on official data, and the brief describes rewards for top strategies.
What is missing

How to find out
Read https://www.nba.com/termsofuse and, if the reward structure is real money, take one hour of legal advice. Record the conclusion in the repository so the next session does not re-litigate it.

q-03 · Is S1 real, or is it the best of six lottery tickets? P1

What we know
23-9, p = 0.020 against a coin flip, Wilson lower bound 0.546. It fails calibration and stability.
What is missing
A corrected significance figure and a pre-registered replication. BH across the six tested strategies gives q = 0.120, and S1's win rate barely changes with edge size (r = 0.051).
Why it decides
It is the difference between 'one strategy shows a signal worth funding at quarter size' and 'nothing has demonstrated skill yet'.
How to find out
Pre-register S1 exactly as it stands, then grade the next 60 games with no changes. At 32 games the design can only detect a very large edge; about 250-300 graded picks are needed to separate 55% from 52.4% at 80% power.

q-04 · What is the true injury-driven minutes and production impact, and how do we keep it current? P1

What we know
The injury layer exists and is rule-based, but it is built on the 04:15 AM edition on a game day. Twenty-two designations changed by 1:30 PM. Minutes are known for only 24 players in the repository.
What is missing
A per-team minutes distribution (all rotation players, not the top 24 league-wide), and an ingestion path that picks up the latest edition instead of a hand-captured one.
Why it decides
The brief asks specifically for injury-driven rotation and production analysis. Right now the impact number is a lower bound computed over a small slice of players, on a stale report.
How to find out
Pull each team's official roster minutes from the Players Traditional table filtered by team (30 requests, all official), then re-price every game against the latest edition and compare before/after.

q-07 · For player props, what exactly is the 'line' being bet against? P2

What we know
Prop lines are the players' official season averages, sometimes one game stale, and the model's projection is that average adjusted for pace and opponent.
What is missing
A stated definition. A season average is not a market line: it has no juice, no half-point, and no closing value.
Why it decides
With an average as the line, an 'over' pick is close to a coin flip by construction unless the adjustment is genuinely predictive. That is why S7 and S8 have zero graded plays.
How to find out
Either define a fixed convention (e.g. 'line = season average rounded to 0.5, graded at even money') and publish it, or drop prop picks until a real line source exists.

q-08 · How should correlated legs be priced across a parlay? P2

What we know
Thirteen correlations among official team stats are measured and reproduce exactly. The same-game-parlay rho values are model guesses (0.0, 0.25, 0.30, -0.20, 0.05) and the site labels them as guesses.
What is missing
A measured rho for any parlay pair that involves a player, which needs a player game-log corpus. The repository holds game logs for 3 players.
Why it decides
Parlay pricing is the whole of the brief's 'parlay and prop combo' section. With guessed rho, a parlay price is a guess with extra steps.
How to find out
Collect official player game logs for the 60-game window (one request per player per season via the official stats pages), then measure pairwise rho directly instead of assuming it.

q-09 · Is the rest signal real, and can we trust the field that carries it? P2

What we know
The repository reports a rest-day differential worth about +2.82 points per game at p = 0.130, needing roughly 340 games. The field itself is currently computed wrongly in the horizon build (26 of 27 rows negative).
What is missing
A correct rest table across the season, and enough games to detect an effect that small.
Why it decides
Rest is one of the few signals that is not just a re-expression of team quality, so it is worth getting right — but only after the field is correct.
How to find out
Fix the rest computation (F-12), widen the corpus to the full season from the official schedule, then re-estimate.

P3 — worth knowing, no decision depends on it yet

q-10 · Which official injury designations actually move a line? P3

What we know
Statuses are mapped to weights (Out 1.0, Doubtful, Questionable 0.35, Probable, Available) and to a 15 net-rating-points-per-100-possessions impact if a full team of minutes were lost.
What is missing
Any measured relationship between a designation and the player's actual minutes. The weights are asserted, not fitted.
Why it decides
If 'Questionable' means a 70% chance of playing 34 minutes, weighting it at 0.35 mis-prices the game. Nothing in the repository tests this.
How to find out
Join the official injury reports (every edition for a fortnight) to the box scores of those games and measure realised minutes by designation. That is a self-contained, entirely official dataset.
